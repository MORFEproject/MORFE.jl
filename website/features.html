Method · Why MORFE

Direct Parametrisation of Invariant Manifolds.

DPIM computes nonlinear normal modes and their reduced dynamics in one shot, working on the assembled FE operators directly. No basis selection. No POD snapshots. No surrogate fitting.

Reduced $\mathbf{z}$-coordinates on the left flow under $\dot{\mathbf{z}} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{z})$. The lift $\mathbf{W}:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^N$ embeds them onto the invariant manifold inside the full FE space on the right.

METHOD · 01

Parametrise, don’t project

Most ROMs project the dynamics onto a linear subspace and pretend the rest is small. DPIM searches for an invariant manifold $\mathcal{W}$ tangent to a chosen master subspace and seeks a smooth parametrisation $\mathbf{W}: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^N$

$$ \mathbf{u}(t) = \mathbf{W}\!\left(\mathbf{z}(t)\right), \qquad \dot{\mathbf{z}} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{z}) $$

Both $\mathbf{W}$ and $\mathbf{f}$ are computed as polynomial expansions in $\mathbf{z}$, order by order, satisfying invariance exactly at every order.

METHOD · 02

Solve the homological equation

At each order $p$, MORFE solves the homological equation arising from the invariance condition:

$$ \mathcal{L}\,\mathbf{W}_p \;-\; \mathbf{W}_p\,\mathbf{D}\mathbf{f}_1 \;=\; \mathcal{R}_p\!\left(\mathbf{W}_{< p}, \mathbf{f}_{< p}\right) $$

The right-hand side depends only on lower orders. Resonant monomials (where the small-divisor blows up) are absorbed into $\mathbf{f}$ — the reduced dynamics — yielding a normal form.

METHOD · 03

Resolve internal resonances

When $p\,\omega_i \pm q\,\omega_j \approx \omega_k$, the corresponding monomial cannot be eliminated and stays in the ROM. MORFE detects these resonances automatically from the spectrum and the user-declared tolerance. The result is a ROM that captures, e.g., the 1:2 internal resonance of an arch or the 1:3 of a beam without manual intervention.

METHOD · 04

Forced & non-autonomous

Non-autonomous forcing is encoded as an ExternalSystem: a finite-dimensional autonomous ODE $\dot{\mathbf{r}} = \boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{r} + \text{h.o.t.}$ whose state $\mathbf{r} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_\text{ext}}$ enters the nonlinear terms of the full-order model as a driving argument. For single-frequency periodic forcing, $\boldsymbol{\Lambda} = \operatorname{diag}(\pm i\Omega)$ so that $\mathbf{r}(t) = e^{\pm i\Omega t}$; the framework extends naturally to multi-frequency quasi-periodic excitation by including additional eigenvalue pairs.

$$ \dot{\mathbf{r}} = \boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{r} + \text{h.o.t.}, \quad \mathbf{r} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_\text{ext}}, \qquad \dot{\mathbf{z}} = \mathbf{f}(\mathbf{z}, \mathbf{r}) $$

The eigenvalues of $\boldsymbol{\Lambda}$ enter the cohomological equations as external superharmonic frequencies. Resonant monomials in the joint $(\mathbf{z}, \mathbf{r})$ space are detected and absorbed into the reduced dynamics on the same footing as internal resonances — no averaging, no Floquet transformation required.

METHOD · 05

Sparse FE-native

The nonlinear operators $\mathbf{G}_{ijk} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N \times N}$ and $\mathbf{H}_{ijkl} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N \times N \times N}$ are never assembled or stored. Instead, each nonlinear term is a FEMMultilinearMap that exposes element-level primitives: scatter_qp! interpolates a $\mathbf{W}$-column to quadrature-point gradients, accumulate_qp! evaluates the multilinear integrand at each quadrature point, and assemble_element! scatters the element residual to global DOFs.

During the DPIM solve, a single element loop per monomial deduplicates scatter operations across terms: if multiple terms share a $\mathbf{W}$-column, it is interpolated to quadrature-point gradients only once per element. The element-local working buffer is $\mathcal{O}(n_\text{unique} \times n_\text{qp})$ — independent of $N^3$ or $N^4$.
