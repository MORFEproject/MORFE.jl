Parametric Kármán profile

The circle obstacle becomes a Joukowski family. The stored mesh is the middle profile, so moving the shape parameter away from zero means exactly what it should: moving away from the geometry actually assembled.

Evidence boundary

The generated dataset now contains the centered geometry certificate, the five accepted direct-FOM Hopf reference points, and the measured Reynolds coordinate. The Reynolds slider and continuous solver curves remain disabled because geometry-order, mesh, quadrature, independent-remesh, production-ROM, and DNS gates are still open. The values shown at the five exact sample positions are evidence points, not interpolated ROM predictions.

Why $t=0.5$ is zero

The Joukowski homotopy is $H_t(\zeta)=\zeta+t c^2/\zeta$, with $c=1$, $\zeta_c=-0.03884761+0.1449935065756745i$, and $R=|1-\zeta_c|$. The negative real offset puts the rounded leading edge at minimum $x$, upstream for the left-to-right flow, while the formal cusp is downstream. We store the mesh at $t_\mathrm{ref}=0.5$ and define

$$\mu=t-0.5,\qquad x(X,\mu)=X+\mu\psi(X).$$

Consequently $x(X,0)=X$, $F_0=I$, and $\det F_0=1$ exactly. At the midpoint, the resolved chord is 0.1 m, its midpoint is $(0.2,0.2)$ m, maximum chord-normal thickness is 37.10%, and maximum centreline camber distance is 6%. “Curvature” on this page always means that camber distance—not differential curvature $\kappa$.

How chord, thickness, and camber are measured

Leading and trailing points are global streamwise extrema refined from roots of $d\operatorname{Re}(H_t)/d\phi=0$. In the resulting chord frame, chord-normal lines intersect the closed boundary to give $y_u(s)$ and $y_l(s)$. Then $h=\max_s(y_u-y_l)$ and $f=\max_s|(y_u+y_l)/2|$. Plot samples never define the endpoints or acceptance values.

Move one topology, do not remesh the ROM

The topology originates from a circular obstacle mesh, but the stored finite-element reference is the moved midpoint profile itself. The analytic obstacle displacement is extended through the fluid by a weighted P1 linear-elasticity problem, with zero mesh displacement on `Inlet`, `Outlet`, and `Farfield`. Physical flow conditions are different: `Inlet` and `Farfield` prescribe the uniform freestream $(1,0)$ m/s, only `Obstacle` is no-slip, and `Outlet` uses natural traction.

motion = generate_reference_mesh(meshfile; level = MESH_LEVELS.FAST)
certificate = certify_geometry(motion)

# reference triangles carry a constant gradient
Fμ = I + μ * ∇ψ
jμ = det(Fμ)
Aμ = adj(Fμ)

Navier–Stokes on the fixed midpoint domain

Velocity uses the composition pullback. There is no Piola transformation. With $A_\mu=\operatorname{adj}F_\mu$ and $j_\mu=\det F_\mu$, the mixed forms are

$$m_\mu(v,\dot u)=\int v\cdot\dot u\,j_\mu\,dX,$$ $$c_\mu(v;a,b)=\int v\cdot[(\nabla_Xb\,A_\mu)a],dX,$$ $$a_{\mu,\xi}(v,u)=2\nu(\xi)\int\frac{\mathrm{sym}(\nabla_XvA_\mu):\mathrm{sym}(\nabla_XuA_\mu)}{j_\mu},dX,$$ $$b_\mu(q,u)=\int q\,\mathrm{tr}(\nabla_XuA_\mu),dX.$$

The second frozen coordinate normalises inverse Reynolds number: $\xi=(1/\mathrm{Re}-1/\mathrm{Re}_0)/\Delta_\eta$, with $\dot\mu=\dot\xi=0$. The five-sample direct-FOM study determines $\Delta_\eta$ with a 10% margin; it is not guessed in configuration.

Why changing geometry raises the model to `ORD = 2`

The pressure mass remains singular, but the velocity mass now depends on geometry: $B_1(\mu)\dot U$. MORFE therefore receives the augmented linear tuple $(B_{0,0},B_{1,0},0)$. Geometry corrections to $B_1$ have derivative arity `(0, 1)`; treating this as an order-one model would attach $\mu$ to $U$ instead of $\dot U$.

pcase = parametric_model(case, geometry;
    geometry_parameter_basis = GeometryParameterBasis([8]),
    reynolds_scale = Δη)

# external order is fixed: r = (μ, ξ)
# conjugate permutation for one Hopf pair: [2, 1, 3, 4]
(; model, spectral, meta) = build_model(pcase; spectrum, master = [1])

Pure-parameter forcing is assembled from the full midpoint state before free-row restriction, so contributions from the prescribed uniform-freestream degrees of freedom are retained. The implementation rejects the model unless $\|h(0,0)\|\le10^{-10}\max(1,\|s_0\|)$.

Full transformed forces

Lift and drag use pressure and viscous stress. Nanson’s formula carries the normal and boundary measure together:

$$\sigma=-pI+2\nu\,\mathrm{sym}(\nabla_xu),\qquad n\,d\Gamma=A_\mu^T N\,d\Gamma_\mathrm{ref}.$$

The implementation’s `compute_transformed_drag_lift` evaluates this on the reference obstacle. A pressure-only lift polynomial is not reused.
