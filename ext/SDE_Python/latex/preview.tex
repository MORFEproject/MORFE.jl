\section*{It\^o versus Stratonovich}

The two integrals differ only in where the integrand is evaluated inside a step.
Figure~\ref{fig:path} shows the sample path and the two evaluation points;
figure~\ref{fig:integrals} shows what the choice accumulates to.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includestandalone{ito_path}
  \caption{(a) A Brownian path at $\Delta t=10^{-4}$ (pale) with the integration
    grid it is sampled on, $\Delta t=10^{-2}$ (black), laid over it. (b) The
    first ten steps of that grid. The It\^o rule evaluates the integrand at the
    left end of each step, $W(t_k)$ (\textcolor{itoblue}{blue circles}); the
    Stratonovich rule takes the mean of the two ends,
    $\tfrac{1}{2}[W(t_k)+W(t_{k+1})]$ (\textcolor{stratred}{red squares}), which
    is the midpoint of the chord and not a value of the path. The dotted segments
    join each pair.}
  \label{fig:path}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includestandalone{ito_integrals}
  \caption{(c) $\int_0^t W\,\mathrm{d}W$ read as It\^o
    (\textcolor{itoblue}{blue}) and as Stratonovich (\textcolor{stratred}{red}),
    each drawn on the integration grid (thin) and, pale behind it, on the fine
    grid, against the closed forms $(W^2-t)/2$ and $W^2/2$ (dashed). The
    Stratonovich sum reproduces the chain rule on any grid; the It\^o sum sits
    off its closed form by $\tfrac{1}{2}(\sum \Delta W^2 - t)$. (d) The gap the
    two accumulate (\textcolor{diffgreen}{green}), against $t/2$ (dashed).}
  \label{fig:integrals}
\end{figure}
